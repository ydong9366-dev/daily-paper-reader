# Abstract 

Designing effective multi-agent systems (MAS) remains a challenging and resource-intensive task. We propose a Bayesian Evolutionary Optimization framework for automatically discovering task-specific MAS topologies. The approach combines evolutionary operators to generate diverse workflow graphs with Bayesian opti-mization to focus cost intensive evaluations on promising candidates. In contrast to LLM-based candidate selection, our approach uses a graph-aware Gaussian Process and Expected Improvement to provide an explicit selection criterion during evolu-tionary search. We evaluate the method on BigCodeBench for code generation and on a tabular preprocessing benchmark using TabPFN. On BigCodeBench, evolved architectures achieve the highest overall performance among the evaluated agent systems. On temporal tabular datasets, optimized MAS preprocessing improves over the default TabPFN model on several tasks, while remaining competitive on IID datasets. Overall, the results demonstrate the potential of Bayesian selection for guiding evolutionary MAS search under expensive evaluation budgets. 

# 1 Introduction 

Multi-agent systems (MAS) have demonstrated strong capabilities across a wide range of complex tasks, including coding and data analysis [1, 2]. By combining multiple specialized agents, MAS can leverage complementary expertise to solve problems that are often beyond the capabilities of a single agent. However, different tasks may benefit from different agent roles and workflow structures, making it challenging and resource-intensive to manually design an effective, task-specific MAS topology [3, 4]. Recent research has shown the potential of combining large language models (LLMs) with evolutionary algorithms. In these approaches, LLMs are used to generate and refine candidate solutions, while evolutionary algorithms provide an efficient mechanism for exploring the search space and discovering increasingly effective system designs [5]. To address the high cost of manually designing task-specific multi-agent systems, we propose a 

Bayesian Evolutionary Optimization method for automated MAS design. Our approach com-bines evolutionary algorithms to generate and diversify candidate MAS topologies with Bayesian optimization to efficiently identify the most promising architectures for evaluation.

# 2 Related Work 

The automated design and optimization of multi-agent systems (MAS) has recently gained significant research interest. ADAS [9] uses a meta-agent to iteratively improve existing systems and generate new MAS architectures. By building on previously discovered high-performing systems, ADAS outperforms conventional prompting and manually designed agent systems [9]. MASS [10] similarly optimizes MAS iteratively by separately optimizing graph topologies and agent prompts before combining them. It achieves strong performance compared to other automated frameworks such as AFLOW [11]. Evolutionary approaches have also been applied to MAS optimization. EVOAGENT [12] evolves MAS topologies through mutation and crossover, with an LLM guiding candidate selection, and out-performs prompting strategies and agent frameworks such as AgentVerse and AutoAgent. EVOMAS [13] combines evolutionary operators with LLM-based selection and achieves superior performance over single-agent systems, handcrafted architectures, and other automated methods across bench-marks including SWE-bench and WorkBench. Beyond LLM-based MAS optimization, GAPPO [14] integrates genetic algorithms with reinforcement learning through mutation, crossover, and selection. While existing evolutionary MAS approaches primarily rely on LLM-based selection, we investigate Bayesian optimization as an alternative selection mechanism. Rather than asking an LLM to judge candidate architectures, our method explicitly models previously evaluated graph topologies and uses Expected Improvement to select candidates for evaluation. This provides an explicit, model-based selection criterion, reducing reliance on stochastic LLM-based judgments.

# 4 Experimental Setup 

We evaluated our method on two benchmarks. First, we developed a custom tabular benchmark to assess whether MAS optimization can improve preprocessing for downstream prediction. The objective is to optimize the preprocessing pipeline such that the resulting data maximizes the predictive information available to the model. Second, we evaluated our approach on BigCodeBench [19] as a traditional MAS benchmark. As baselines, we considered a Single Agent system and a manually designed Planner → Executor → Validator MAS. Across all experiments, we used Gemma 4 31B [20] as both the meta-model and the underlying language model for all agents. To improve reproducibility, we fixed the sampling temperature to 0.25 [21, 22]. All benchmarks used separate training and test sets.

# 6 Conclusion 

In this project, we proposed a novel approach for automatically discovering task-specific multi-agent system (MAS) topologies by combining evolutionary algorithms with Bayesian optimization. The evolved architectures achieve higher average performance than both a Single Agent baseline and a manually designed multi-agent system architecture on BigCodeBench and showed competitive performance on the tabular data benchmark. 

Limitations and Future Work A key limitation is that topology optimization and prompt gen-eration are optimized separately. While Bayesian optimization guides topology selection, prompt assignment may still affect final performance. Moreover, the GED-based similarity is not guaranteed to be positive semi-definite, which may affect the behavior of the Bayesian selection (see Appendix A). Future work could jointly optimize topologies and prompts, for example by incorporating prompt optimization methods such as Cantante [24]. Further evaluation across a broader range of tasks and domains is needed to assess the generalizability of the approach.
